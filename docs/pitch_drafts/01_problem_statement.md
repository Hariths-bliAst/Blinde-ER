## Slide 2: The Problem Statement
- **2.2 Billion People** globally live with near or distant vision impairment (WHO), yet over 90% of printed books are never produced in accessible formats.
- **Robotic, Literal OCR-to-Speech:** Existing scanning apps translate text word-for-word with flat, robotic prosody—reading out broken hyphenations (`"Northum- berland"`), stray page headers, and footnotes that ruin comprehension.
- **Lost Emotion in Translation:** Standard machine translation ignores literary genre, idiom, and emotional tone.
- **Inaccessible UIs:** Most OCR apps require aiming at tiny on-screen shutter buttons, creating a frustrating barrier for blind users.
- **Video Narration Script (0:20 – 0:50):**
  > *"Today, standard OCR-to-speech tools fail blind readers in three critical ways. First, they read raw OCR literally—speaking broken line-hyphenations and page numbers out loud. Second, literal translation strips away the emotional tone and genre nuance of a book. And third, most apps rely on tiny visual buttons that require sight just to press."*
